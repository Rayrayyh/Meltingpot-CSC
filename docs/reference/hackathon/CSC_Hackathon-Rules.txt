About the Challenge
The CSC Back-to-School Hackathon is a beginner-friendly high school hackathon where students build technology to improve school life.

Your project can be an app, website, tool, AI project, design prototype, or physical gadget. It can help students, teachers, clubs, classrooms, dorms, teams, or schools solve a real problem. Projects can focus on studying, scheduling, communication, organization, wellness, accessibility, campus life, classroom tools, or any other school-related challenge.

AI tools are allowed and encouraged. You may use tools such as ChatGPT, Claude, or other coding and design assistants to brainstorm, code, debug, design, and build your project. Using AI will not lower your score. The goal is to help students explore ideas, build higher-quality projects, and see how computer science can create practical change.

However, teams must disclose how they used AI and must be able to explain their project, their decisions, and how their final product works.

Get Started
1. Find a real school-life problem.
2. Think about who the problem affects and why it matters.
3. Build an app, website, AI tool, prototype, or gadget that helps solve it.
4. Use any tools, tutorials, APIs, templates, datasets, or AI assistants that help you learn and build.
5. Submit your project on Devpost with an explanation, demo or other evidence of your project, and AI-use disclosure.

You may work individually or in teams of up to 4 people. A 1–2 minute demo video is optional but encouraged.

Requirements
What to Build
Build an app, website, tool, AI project, or physical gadget that helps students, teachers, or schools solve a real school-life problem. Your project does not need to be perfect or fully finished, but it should clearly show the idea, how it works, and why it would be useful. 


What to Submit
Each team must submit:
- Project name
- Short description of the problem you are solving
- Explanation of what your project does
- Demo link, website link, video, screenshots, photos, or another way for judges to understand the project
- List of tools, technologies, APIs, datasets, hardware, or AI tools used
- AI-use disclosure explaining how your team used AI during the project
- Team member names
- Source code, build files, or design files if available

For physical gadgets, submit photos or a short demo video showing the prototype. For apps, websites, and AI tools, submit a demo, GitHub repository, screenshots, or 1–2 minute video that clearly demonstrates the project.

Judging Criteria
Learning
Can the team clearly identify what they built, why they built it, how it works, and what they learned? AI tools are allowed and will not lower anyone's score, but disclosure must reveal the use of AI and show understanding of the final product.
Design
Is the project easy to understand and use? A strong project will have a clear interface, thoughtful design, simple user flow, and practical use cases.
Creativity
Is the idea thoughtful, original, or interesting? A strong project does not need to be complicated, but it should show a clear, non-generic approach to improving school life.
Functionality
How well does the project work? A strong project should demonstrate the core idea through a working app, website, AI tool, gadget, design prototype, demo, or clear proof of concept.
Impact
Does the project address a real school-life problem? A strong project will clearly identify who the problem affects and explain why the solution would be useful for students, teachers, clubs, classrooms, or schools.
